## What the song is saying
**One sentence:** a shy man who adores a woman from a distance: first in private devotion, then in open, joyful praise.
He hasn't told her yet.

**1. Love that hasn't been spoken.** "Your eyes speak" opens the song. The first conversation is in looks, not words. Everything
in the verse is what *he* sees and feels; she never answers. The bridge confirms it: "*if* I could meet you, *then* I'd
tell you." The confession is conditional and still in the future. The emotional engine of the whole song is this gap
between what he feels and what he has said.

**2. Devotion, not just attraction.** He reaches for sacred images, not physical ones:
- **મૂર્તિ (idol):** she is like a temple image, beheld with reverence, a little out of reach.
- **તુલસી in the આંગણ (tulsi in the courtyard):** this is the key image of the song. The tulsi plant stands at the centre of a
  Gujarati home's courtyard, worshipped every morning and evening with water and a diya; it is auspicious and it is *home*.
  Saying "your presence is the tulsi of my courtyard" means: you make my home holy, you belong at the centre of my life.
  It is a marriage image without saying marriage.

**3. From "તું" to "તમે".** The verses address her as **તું** (intimate, inner voice, as you'd speak in your own head).
The chorus switches to **તમે** ("તમે તો સુંદર"), the respectful, public form. The song moves from private longing to
open celebration, as if he finally says it out loud, to the whole village.

**4. The chorus is about movement.** **નમણી** means slender, delicate, graceful; **ચાલ** is the way she walks. The hook is not
her face but her *walk*, with the details of a festive Gujarati look: the **કંદોરો** (silver waist chain) and roses in her
hair. These belong to celebration: Navratri, a wedding, a fair. The chorus is the song's happiest, most physical part.

**Arc:** reverent gaze → sacred image (idol, tulsi) → shy wish to confess → open, joyful praise of her as she walks.
